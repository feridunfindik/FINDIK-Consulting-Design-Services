Step 1: The Winning Formula

The Golden 4-Paragraph Template

Paragraph 1 · Introduction ~18–22 words

State your opinion clearly (do not copy the question word-for-word), then say you have two reasons.

I think that [topic is good / bad].

I have two reasons for this.

Or: "I do not think that..."
Paragraph 2 · Reason 1 + Example ~25–30 words

Use POINT #1 in your first sentence. Add a second sentence with an example or explanation.

First, [Reason 1 using Point #1].

For example, [specific detail or result].

Detail starter: "For example," / "This is because"
Paragraph 3 · Reason 2 + Detail ~25–30 words

Use POINT #2 in your first sentence. Follow up with why this matters.

Second, [Reason 2 using Point #2].

This is because [explanation / result].

Or: "In addition," / "As a result,"
Paragraph 4 · Conclusion ~15–18 words

Repeat your opinion using slightly different words (e.g., change I think to I believe or I disagree).

For these reasons, I believe that [restate opinion].

Keep it 1 strong sentence!

Your Draft vs. Passing Model Essay

Prompt: "Some people say that online shopping is better than shopping at physical stores. Do you agree?"

What we fixed:
  • ✕ Length: 52 words needs ~35 more words (add 1 detail sentence to each reason).
  • ✕ Layout: Written as 1 big block with comma splices instead of 4 separated paragraphs.
  • ✕ Spelling & Grammar: a online → an online; shoping → shopping; physicall → physical.

Passing Model Revision

87 Words (Sweet Spot!)

Intro I do not think that online shopping is better than shopping at physical stores. I have two reasons for this.

Reason 1 First, online shops do not have the fun of shopping. At physical stores, people can walk around and see new items in person.

Reason 2 Second, when you shop at an online store, it is easy to lose track of what you bought. You might order too many things without noticing.

Conclusion For these reasons, I disagree with the idea that online shopping is better than physical shopping.

Why this gets a high score:
  • ✓ Uses YOUR exact ideas, just expanded with bolded supporting examples.
  • ✓ Clean 4-paragraph visual layout makes it effortless for the Eiken grader to read.
  • ✓ 87 words lands right in the middle of the 80–100 word target zone.

Step 4: Avoid Easy Point Losses

Top 5 Trap Points & Spelling Watchlist

1

Don't Say "I don't agree to..."

In English, you cannot say "I don't agree to [sentence]". Use clean patterns instead:

I don't agree to online shopping is better.
✓ I do not think that online shopping is better.
✓ I disagree with this idea.
2

Watch Your "a" vs. "an"

Before words starting with a vowel sound (a, e, i, o, u), always switch a to an—or use plural nouns!

in a online shop
✓ in an online shop
✓ at online shops (plural is super safe!)
3

High-Frequency Spelling Traps

Double letters and silent letters often appear on Eiken prompts. Check these before finishing:

shopping(double p)
physical(single l)
environment(i-r-o-n)
convenient(i before e)
4

Never Skip the Support Sentence

Writing only 1 sentence per reason leaves your essay around 50 words. Always add a 2nd sentence:

For example, students can learn...
This is because it takes too much time.
As a result, people can save money.
5

Use the Prompt's POINTS Keywords

Eiken Grade 2 gives you 3 bulleted keywords (POINTS) below the question. You must pick 2 of them and connect your reasons to those ideas—otherwise your Content score drops significantly.

If POINT is "Cost": Talk about saving money, expensive prices, or free services.
If POINT is "Health": Talk about exercise, stress, sleep, or eating habits.
If POINT is "Time": Talk about busy schedules, saving time, or studying more.
