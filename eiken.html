Step 1: The Winning Formula

The Golden 4-Paragraph Template

Paragraph 1 · Introduction ~18–22 words

State your opinion clearly (do not copy the question word-for-word), then say you have two reasons.

I think that [topic is good / bad].

I have two reasons for this.

Or: "I do not think that..."
Paragraph 2 · Reason 1 + Example ~25–30 words

Use POINT #1 in your first sentence. Add a second sentence with an example or explanation.

First, [Reason 1 using Point #1].

For example, [specific detail or result].

Detail starter: "For example," / "This is because"
Paragraph 3 · Reason 2 + Detail ~25–30 words

Use POINT #2 in your first sentence. Follow up with why this matters.

Second, [Reason 2 using Point #2].

This is because [explanation / result].

Or: "In addition," / "As a result,"
Paragraph 4 · Conclusion ~15–18 words

Repeat your opinion using slightly different words (e.g., change I think to I believe or I disagree).

For these reasons, I believe that [restate opinion].

Keep it 1 strong sentence!

3. Polished Passing Model

88 Words

Intro I do not think that online shopping is better than shopping at physical stores. I have two reasons for this.

Reason 1 First of all, online shops do not have the fun of shopping. At physical stores, you can see the items by yourself. Also, you can explore and find new things.

Reason 2 Second of all, when you use online shopping, you can lose track of what you bought and accidentally buy too much stuff.

Conclusion For these reasons, I disagree with the idea that online shopping is better than physical stores.

Exam-ready: 4 clean paragraphs with zero spelling or verb-form deductions.

Smartphones Passing Model Revision

94 Words (Target Hit!)

Intro I disagree with this idea. I have two reasons for this.

Reason 1 First of all, recently we have many ways to use social media, like Instagram and TikTok. However, these apps can sometimes be dangerous because young people might get involved in crimes.

Reason 2 Second of all, using smartphones too much might cause neck injuries and a lack of studying. For example, students often stay up late watching videos instead of doing homework.

Conclusion Therefore, I think that junior high school students having their own smartphones is not a good idea.

Step 4: Your Personal Watchlist

Top 6 Trap Points & Personal Spelling Checklist

Every habit below comes directly from your three practice drafts. Review these 6 cards in the final 2 minutes of the exam!

1

"shopping" (Double p Every Time!)

You spelled shopping right in Sentence 1, then wrote shoping 4 times later. Always scan every sh-o-p-p-i-n-g in your final 2-minute check!

online shoping / fun of shoping
✓ online shopping / fun of shopping
2

Plural "shops don't" vs. "doesn't"

Plural nouns (online shops) take don't. And when using singular, spell doesn't with o-e (never dosen't):

online stopes dosen't have...
✓ online shops don't have...
✓ an online shop doesn't have...
3

Parallel Verbs After "can"

When two verbs share can (can A and B), both verbs stay in base form—never switch the second verb to -ing:

you can lose track ... and acidentaly buying
✓ you can lose track ... and accidentally buy
4

"so much stuff" (Uncountable)

Stuff is spelled without an a and is uncountable—so pair it with much (not many):

buying so many stauff
✓ buy so much stuff / too much stuff
5

"I disagree with the idea that..."

Never write "disagree to" or "disagree to think". Memorize this exact closing chunk:

I disagree to think online shoping is...
✓ I disagree with the idea that [topic]...
6

Quick Spelling & Spacing Checks

Your top 6 high-frequency words to double-check before putting your pencil down:

things(with h, not tings)
yourself(1 word, not your self)
accidentally(double c, double l)
two reasons(number 2, not to)
injuries / studying(-ies / keep y)
